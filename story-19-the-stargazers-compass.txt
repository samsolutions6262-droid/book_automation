The Stargazer's Compass
=======================

On the wind-scoured cliffs of Cape Solitude, old Elias spent sixty winters
crafting nautical instruments for merchant captains. His brass sextants were
precise to a second of arc, his compasses balanced upon polished sapphires.

Yet in a velvet-lined drawer beneath his workbench rested a brass compass that
pointed toward nothing on nautical charts.

Its needle spun lazily, indifferent to Earth's magnetic north. When young
sailors visited his workshop, they laughed at the peculiar trinket.

"A broken toy, Master Elias?" one asked, tapping the glass. "What good is a
needle that refuses north?"

"North is easy," Elias replied without glancing up from his lathe. "North is
where ice accumulates and iron sleeps. But this needle turns toward whichever
direction holds the thing you left behind and cannot forget."

The young sailor smirked and took the compass in his palm. The needle hesitated,
shivered, and swung decisively southeast — straight toward the orchards of his
inland home, where a girl had asked him not to take to sea.

The young man's grin vanished. He gently laid the compass back upon the velvet
and walked out into the harbor rain without uttering another syllable.

Elias closed the drawer with a soft click. He never looked at the compass himself.
At his age, he already knew which way the needle pointed.

                            THE END
