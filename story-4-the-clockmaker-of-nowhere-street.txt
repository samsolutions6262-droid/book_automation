The Clockmaker of Nowhere Street
================================

Nobody could find Nowhere Street on a map, which suited the
clockmaker fine. Customers came to him only by getting lost in
exactly the right way.

He fixed two kinds of clocks: the kind that told time, and the
kind that told something else. The second kind was the harder
business, but the more frequent.

A woman brought him a kitchen clock that ran four minutes fast
every day, no matter the adjustment. He opened it, and found a
small brass wheel engraved with a recipe in a dead hand. "It's
fast," he explained, "because it's counting down to something,
not up from something. Feed whoever wrote that recipe. Fixes
half the clocks I see."

A boy brought a wristwatch that stopped every time he lied. The
clockmaker did not repair it. He simply sat the boy down, gave
him tea, and waited, and told no one what the boy said first.
The watch never stopped again.

An old man brought in a grandfather clock that told the hours of
someone else's life entirely — unmarked, unlabelled, chiming at
strange moments. The clockmaker opened the case, found a nursery
rhyme folded behind the pendulum, and handed it back without a word,
along with the clock, now silent and correct.

"You didn't fix anything," the old man said.

"Correct," said the clockmaker, reaching for the next case.

                            THE END
