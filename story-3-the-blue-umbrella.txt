The Blue Umbrella
=================

The blue umbrella had been left outside the corner bakery for three
days, and nobody took it.

It wasn't for lack of rain. It rained sideways every afternoon that
week. People simply stepped around it, the way people step around a
sleeping dog, with a vague respectful sorrow.

On the fourth day, Priya — who was late, who was always late, who had
given up on every umbrella she had ever owned because they always
ended up in someone else's hands — saw it, sighed, and decided to
steal it openly. She picked it up and walked.

It turned out to be the finest umbrella she had ever held. The canopy
never inverted. The ribs did not rattle. Rain struck it and sounded
like polite applause. And every person she passed under it with
seemed, somehow, a little less hurried.

"You left this outside the bakery," the baker told her that evening,
as she returned it to the awning where she'd found it. "Three days.
It's a loaner. It belongs to the street."

"To the street?"

"We had a poet live above us in the nineties. Left four umbrellas
when she moved out. Blue for strangers, red for friends. It's still
managed by nobody and works perfectly."

Priya looked at the umbrella drying in the doorway, faithful as a
lamppost, and understood she had not stolen it at all. She had been
lent something, briefly, that had no owner — only a pattern of care,
passed hand to hand by strangers who agreed about nothing else.

                            THE END
