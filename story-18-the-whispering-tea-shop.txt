The Whispering Tea Shop
=======================

At the corner of Weaver Alley, where cobblestones met the morning mist,
sat Master Lin's modest tea establishment. It lacked neon signs and glossy
menus, yet travelers from distant provinces often arrived before dawn.

They did not come for rare tea leaves or porcelain teacups. They came
because Lin possessed an unusual gift: he brewed tea to match what a heart
kept unsaid.

To the young scholar weighed down by family expectation, Lin poured a cup
of pale mountain oolong that tasted distinctly like a summer meadow after rain.
With the first sip, the boy breathed freely for the first time in years.

To the widow carrying silence like an iron kettle, he brought roasted barley
steeped with dried orange peel. It smelled faintly of woodsmoke and old laughter.
A single tear tracked down her weathered cheek, not of despair, but of sweet,
unburdened memory.

"Master Lin," asked an apprentice one quiet dusk, sweeping tea leaves from the hearth,
"how do you know what herbs to steep when no guest speaks a word of their troubles?"

Lin smiled, swirling hot water in an earthenware pot.

"People speak volumes before they ever open their mouths," he murmured gently.
"In the slump of a shoulder, the cadence of a breath, or how tightly fingers grip
an empty cup. Listening is not an ear's duty, child. It belongs entirely to patience."

And through the small window, dusk fell like steeped amber, quiet and warm.

                            THE END
