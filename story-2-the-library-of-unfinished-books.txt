The Library of Unfinished Books
===============================

Tucked behind a laundromat in the old quarter of the city, there was
a library that lent only manuscripts no one had ever finished.

Novels that stopped at chapter four. Memoirs that ended the day the
author got married. A cookbook with recipes for dishes never once
cooked. The librarian, a small woman named Odile with ink permanently
on her left ring finger, kept the only completed book behind the desk:
a rulebook, one page long.

"Rule," it said. "Finish nothing here. Only begin what you mean to."

Jamal found the library on a rainy Tuesday, having abandoned eleven
novels in eleven drawers. He borrowed a manuscript about a lighthouse
that walked inland on crab legs, read it to its last dangling sentence,
and found himself scribbling the next line in the margin — then the
next paragraph, and then, somehow, months later, the ending.

When he brought it back, whole and finished, Odile didn't put it on
a shelf. She put it in a small wooden box, and the box in a drawer,
and the drawer under the desk with the rulebook.

"What happens to finished ones?" he asked.

"Nothing," she said. "That's rather the point. They're done with us.
The unfinished ones are the ones that need a reader."

                            THE END
