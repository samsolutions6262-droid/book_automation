The Postcard That Refused to Arrive
===================================

The postcard was addressed to Marta Wick, 14 Cordelia Lane, and it
was very bad at its job.

For eleven years it traveled. It went to the wrong Lisbon twice,
spent a winter in the belly of a cargo ship, rode three postal
workers' coat pockets through strikes and holidays, and once, for
reasons no audit could explain, spent four months filed under "W"
in a stationery shop in Graz.

It was written by Marta's brother, the summer before he stopped
writing to anyone. The message was one line:

"The answer is yes, if you're still willing."

Marta had moved four times since then, and the postcard chased her
with the patience of a debt collector. Postal systems failed. Fax
machines died. The internet ate whole decades. But the postcard
persisted, passed hand to hand by clerks who found it in mis-sort
bins and felt, unaccountably, that it mattered.

When it finally slid through the door of 14 Cordelia Lane — which
was now a dental office, because Marta had finally settled two
streets over at number 20 — it arrived on the morning she had
decided, after eleven years of almost, to stop waiting for any
kind of answer about anything.

She read it at her kitchen table, then went to the door of her
own apartment and looked down the hallway as if the postcard
might still be standing there, out of breath.

"Yes," she wrote back, on the same postcard, and mailed it to an
address she had to invent. It arrived, of course. By then they
were both very good at their jobs.

                            THE END
