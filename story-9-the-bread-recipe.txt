The Recipe That Worked Everywhere Except Home
=============================================

Petra's grandmother made bread so reliably extraordinary that a
bakery three towns over licensed the recipe, hired her to consult,
and still could not reproduce it. Neither could Petra, and neither
could the food scientist with the calorimeter and the flour assay.

The ingredients were ordinary. The method was ordinary. The
kneading was, if anything, insufficient.

"It works everywhere except home," the food scientist complained,
on her fourth visit. "Your ratios give us card stock."

"My grandmother lived in six countries and three wars," Petra
said. "You think the recipe is the point?"

The scientist left her instruments running overnight once, out
of spite, and in the morning the log showed the bread changing
oven temperature eleven times. Petra's grandmother had cooked on
a stove with a leaking thermostat for forty years and had never
once called it a problem — she simply stood near, and adjusted,
and knew.

They never cracked it. The bakery settled for excellent instead
of transcendent and did fine. The scientist's paper became one
of the most cited in its field, mostly for its conclusion, which
read:

"The ingredient our instruments cannot detect is practice under
failure. Recommend all future studies hire someone who has been
interrupted."

Petra's grandmother read the paper, laughed her whole hoarse
laugh, and asked for a copy to put in the drawer where she kept
the recipe — which, when Petra finally went through it, contained
forty-three versions, no two alike, all correct.

                            THE END
