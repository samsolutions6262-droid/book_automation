The Lighthouse Keeper's Last Light
==================================

Every night for forty years, Maren climbed the spiral stairs of the
Gullwing Point lighthouse. Forty years of wind that sounded like
whales singing, and a lamp she cleaned until the brass shone like
a cheap mirror.

On the night the service came to retire her, a storm rolled in
early. The inspector's launch was still three miles out when the
electric light failed with a sound like a sigh.

Maren did not think. She lit the old backup flame, the one they
had told her a dozen times to stop maintaining, and she turned the
crank with hands that remembered every motion. For one hour, the
original lighthouse light — the one from 1898 — guided the boats home.

When the inspector finally stumbled up the stairs, soaked and
grateful, he found her seated calmly by the flame, knitting.

"The new lights are brighter," he admitted.

"Mm," said Maren. " brightness and attention are not the same
thing. That ship out there didn't need a spotlight. It needed
someone who never stopped showing up."

The service kept her on for three more years. And every month,
though nobody required it, the old flame was lit for one hour,
just so it would remember how.

                            THE END
