The Garden Under the Highway
============================

No sunlight reached the strip of ground beneath the elevated
highway, and yet by midsummer everything was growing there:
tomatoes, marigolds, beans climbing the concrete pillars,
a lemon tree that had no business being alive.

Lena, twelve years old and scientifically furious, investigated.
The trick, she discovered, was a network of mirrors.

Old men on the adjacent rooftop angled salvaged cabinet doors and
car hubcaps, bouncing light down along the pillars in slow silver
ropes that shifted through the day. The whole garden moved with the
sun without ever seeing it.

"Show me how you figured this out," she demanded of the oldest one,
who was called Uncle Rennick by everyone, including, possibly,
his children.

"Couldn't," he said. "Somebody left the first mirror for us.
Leaning against this pillar in 1979. By the time I asked who had
put it there, there were eleven mirrors and nobody remember which
one was first."

Lena spent the rest of the summer mapping the angles in a
composition book, correcting three of them with a protractor and
a borrowed level. The lemon tree, which had produced four lemons
the year before, produced eleven.

She left the composition book under the best tomato plant when her
family moved away. By the following spring, three morebooks rested
there beside it, and the light had grown a little kinder, and
nobody remembered where any of it had started.

                            THE END
